## Toolbox terminal

Toolbox terminal is a simple cyberdeck built into an old component box. It plays music and runs a terminal.

![The Toolbox terminal open, its screen running beside a keyboard, a Raspberry Pi and a tray of parts.](images/toolbox5.jpg){:width="550px"}

### The enclosure

Toolbox terminal is built into a component box, the kind with a hinged lid and moulded compartments. The compartments hold the parts in place, and the Raspberry Pi can be dropped straight into one of the slots.

![The component box used as the Toolbox terminal case.](images/toolbox-case.jpg){:width="550px"}

### Devices

A screen is attached to the lid with screws. A tiny Bluetooth keyboard is used, because a full-size one would not fit in the box.

![The tiny Bluetooth keyboard used with the Toolbox terminal.](images/toolbox-devices.jpg){:width="550px"}

Speakers are added so it can play music.

![The speakers added so the Toolbox terminal can play music loudly.](images/toolbox-speakers.jpg){:width="550px"}

### Cable management

Holes are cut in the side of the box so the cables can pass through to power and connect the parts, and these are covered with electrical tape.

![Cables routed out through a hole cut in the case.](images/toolbox-cables.jpeg){:width="550px"}

The cables are also taped to the lid to keep them tidy.

![Cables taped down neatly inside the case.](images/toolbox-taped-cables.jpg){:width="550px"}

### Software

The software matches the look and feel of the case. A terminal dashboard shows the system in a retro, cyberpunk style, and it plays music through the speakers.

![The Toolbox terminal cyberpunk-style software running on screen.](images/pideck.gif){:width="550px"}
